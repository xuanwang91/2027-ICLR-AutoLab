Strategic support & national research infrastructure

Built on two complementary pillars.

AutoLab connects a committed scientific evaluation and community partner with an emerging nationwide AI-programmable laboratory network. Together, these offer a pathway from a focused ICLR workshop to sustained benchmarks, experiments and collaborations.

Pillar 02 · National research-infrastructure connection

NSF National PCL Test Bed Network

Virginia Tech is among the NSF-funded teams developing remotely accessible AI-enabled experimental infrastructure. AutoLab's lead organizer is the VT node's AI technical lead.

Virginia Tech node: approximately $20M award
  • National scale: NSF announced an inaugural $380M investment across 20 teams in July 2026 to establish a national network of AI-programmable laboratories.
  • Cross-domain reach: the developing network spans research across science and engineering, connecting institutions and experimental capabilities.
  • Targeted outreach: the VT team will invite scientists across the PCL ecosystem, including academic, industry and public-sector communities.
  • Long-term potential: emerging test beds can help motivate future physical validation, shared evaluations and sustained scientific-community exchange.

Research agenda

Five open problems. One learning loop.

01 / GROUNDING

Grounded scientific reasoning

Connect model-generated hypotheses to measurable scientific evidence and physical constraints.

02 / DECISIONS

Experimental decision-making

Select informative, feasible experiments under limited resources and uncertain outcomes.

03 / FEEDBACK

Learning from physical feedback

Update decisions and models from real observations, including unexpected or failed experiments.

04 / WORLD MODELS

Multimodal scientific world models

Integrate heterogeneous signals, scientific instruments, text, and physical measurements.

05 / RELIABILITY

Reliable autonomy

Build safe, reproducible, auditable scientific agents and experimental workflows.

Chen Institute × AutoLab · Shared Task

From consequential problems to verifiable agent behavior.

With our committed lead sponsor and shared-task collaborator, Tianqiao and Chrissy Chen Institute, AutoLab will organize a scientific-agent competition building on Apodex Discovery / TRACES.

The challenge examines more than final-answer accuracy: evidence grounding, tool and action validity, repair after failed steps, resource awareness and verifiable agent traces. Approximately 50 teams are anticipated; the workshop-specific call, rules and registration will be announced after acceptance.

Beyond a single conference day

A durable community for autonomous science.

AutoLab is designed to turn difficult experimental questions into shared evaluation practice, cross-sector dialogue and reusable community resources—not just one day of talks.

01 / AT ICLR

Open Problems Forum

Attendees bring questions; moderated groups identify concrete bottlenecks, representative failures, testable hypotheses and evaluation criteria.

02 / WITHIN TWO WEEKS

Public Community Roadmap

Organizers synthesize Forum, shared-task and panel outcomes, preserving disagreements and distinguishing evidence from unresolved hypotheses.

03 / LONG-TERM EXCHANGE

Benchmarks, AIAS⁺ & PCL

Maintain public resources, cross-promote through AIAS⁺ and invite connections across the developing NSF PCL national academic, industry and public-research ecosystem.

Confirmed speakers, Chen Institute resources, and direct VT participation in a national autonomous-laboratory initiative give the AutoLab community credible channels for continued engagement beyond ICLR 2027.

Future workshop editions, cross-institution experiments and collaborations remain ambitions rather than formally committed activities.
