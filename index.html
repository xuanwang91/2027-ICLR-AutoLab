AutoLab:AI-Driven Autonomous Laboratories

The next frontier of AI for science is not simply to reason about scientific knowledge, but to learn through interaction with the physical world.

Explore the vision ↗Workshop program
Our central question

What happens when AI decisions become physical experiments?

How should an AI system learn when its decisions become physical experiments, and the resulting observations determine what it does next?

AutoLab focuses on machine-learning principles for closed-loop scientific experimentation across scientific domains, rather than a single application area.

Research agenda

Five open problems. One learning loop.

01 / GROUNDING

Grounded scientific reasoning

Connect model-generated hypotheses to measurable scientific evidence and physical constraints.

02 / DECISIONS

Experimental decision-making

Select informative, feasible experiments under limited resources and uncertain outcomes.

03 / FEEDBACK

Learning from physical feedback

Update decisions and models from real observations, including unexpected or failed experiments.

04 / WORLD MODELS

Multimodal scientific world models

Integrate heterogeneous signals, scientific instruments, text, and physical measurements.

05 / RELIABILITY

Reliable autonomy

Build safe, reproducible, auditable scientific agents and experimental workflows.

Beyond invited talks

A shared task for real-world scientific discovery

In collaboration with the Tianqiao and Chrissy Chen Institute, AutoLab plans a shared task evaluating AI agents for scientific discovery. The goal is to reveal where agents succeed and fail when reasoning must connect to experimental evidence.
